# DoseCam and EBT-XD near 40 Gy

Review of the seven-slide presentation at revision `2323b09`, with emphasis on slides 2 and 3. The question is how precisely DoseCam recovers a film-calibrated peak dose for a single pulse. The October data establish the observed consistency of four paired readings at one dose level. The most relevant external comparison is an independent dosimeter exposed on the same accelerator.

## What the four shots measure

Let `A` be the fitted camera peak in DN and `D` the supplied film peak in Gy. The deployed scale is the ratio of means, `k = ΣA/ΣD = 1.056283 DN/Gy` before rounding. The comparison uses `100 × (A/k/D − 1)`. The scatter quoted throughout this review is the sample standard deviation of the four unscaled ratios `A/D`, divided by their mean. [Archived baseline fits](../20261014/crosscal_fits_PolyAlDosCal_000006.csv) · [Workbook values and ROI correspondence](RESEARCH.md#workbook-audit-and-absolute-dose-uncertainty-2026-11-18)

| Well | Camera peak (DN) | Film peak (Gy) | Difference after fitting scale to all four | Difference with scale fitted to the other three |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| B1 | 42.2357 | 40.9376 | −2.33% | −3.10% |
| A1 | 42.9171 | 39.6129 | +2.57% | +3.44% |
| A2 | 42.4502 | 39.7459 | +1.11% | +1.49% |
| A3 | 41.4009 | 39.7022 | −1.28% | −1.69% |

Fitting the scale forces the sum of the four dose differences in Gy to zero. A uniform change in film dose rescales every reported camera dose and leaves these fractional differences unchanged. The range of the held-out differences illustrates how much each point influences the calibration. All four use the same film calibration and the three-shot training sets overlap. Neither comparison establishes an absolute accuracy or tests response linearity across dose levels.

Four shots also give little precision on the scatter itself. As an illustration only, if the four underlying ratios were independent normal draws, the conventional 95% interval for their population SD, expressed relative to the observed mean, would be about **1.26–8.30%** (three degrees of freedom). The common target and spatial drift limit that assumption. The sample correlation between camera and film is −0.06. Four pairs cannot establish independence or assign the scatter to either instrument. Combining the separate camera and film spreads while ignoring their covariance gives 2.17%. The directly observed paired dispersion is 2.23%. [JCGM uncertainty guide, §5.2](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/2071204/JCGM_100_2008_E.pdf)

### Camera precision and the masking test

The fitter minimizes unweighted DN residuals with a soft-L1 loss. For the nominal peak error it uses the ordinary model Jacobian and the residual mean square, with seven fitted parameters. This gives 0.1602, 0.1664, 0.1480 and 0.1531 DN in pulse order. There is no per-pixel Poisson-plus-read likelihood. The ROI-border noise sets the denominator of the displayed reduced chi-squared. Values near one indicate a reasonable residual scale relative to the border. They cannot establish independent pixels or the coverage of the quoted errors. [Production fitter](../../../dose_cam/beam_fit.py) · [Archived covariance audit](scripts/peak_fit_uncertainty.csv)

The earlier audit projected independent photon counting and measured background fluctuations through the fit, obtaining roughly 0.12–0.13% apiece and about 0.18% combined under its assumed conversion gain. Background here includes multiple sources, including quantization. These are cross-checks of noise already present in the residuals. Neither the 0.18% estimate nor either component should be added to the nominal fit error. The difference between 0.18% and the nominal figure cannot be uniquely attributed to an additional source. [Background and photon calculations](scripts/fit_noise_audit.json)

For this review the production despiker, quadratic local baseline and robust Gaussian fitter were applied to the archived raw frames using the archived fitting regions. At 40 DN the amplitudes reproduce the archived values within 0.00006 DN. Only the despiking threshold changed. The scale was re-estimated from the same four film doses at each threshold to match the intended calibration workflow. [Reproducible fit sensitivity audit](scripts/production_fit_sensitivity_audit.py)

The largest change of an individual calibrated residual from the default was about 0.076 percentage points across the five tested thresholds. Comparing any two thresholds gave a largest span of 0.113 percentage points. Within 30–50 DN, the largest departure from the default was about 0.040 percentage points. A second check grouped the robust-fit residual contributions of neighboring pixels together. Treating individual pixels independently gives 0.30–0.34% with this alternative covariance estimator. Grouping 8 × 8 pixels gives 1.29–1.54%, and 16 × 16 gives 1.93–2.40% across shots and block placements. Grouping can count a repeatable departure from a Gaussian beam profile as though it were a random fluctuation. The recorded frames cannot distinguish those cases or validate a preferred block size. **Keep the published per-fit number nominal.** The block estimates diagnose sensitivity to the independence assumption and provide no separate contribution to add. ### Film readout, target and timing

The four workbook ROIs use EBT-XD lot 06092501 and the same 24-hour curve. The reported intensity SDs are 1.10–1.41% of intensity. Holding the reference intensity fixed and using the local dose/optical-density slope converts them to roughly 1.9–2.4% *dose spread*. The export does not identify whether the SD is over pixels, windows or repeated scans. A spatial SD may include genuine beam gradients and film texture. A standard error of the reported peak and an absolute calibration uncertainty cannot be recovered from the export. The film uses a maximum with a 10-pixel window, while the camera uses a Gaussian peak. Changes in beam shape could affect the two estimators differently even with perfect registration. Their difference has no assigned percentage here. [Workbook audit](RESEARCH.md#workbook-audit-and-absolute-dose-uncertainty-2026-11-18) · [Whitmore et al., peak-estimator comparison](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11096185/)

The maximum pairwise centroid separation is 10.73 camera pixels, between B1 and A3. The often-quoted ~9 pixels describes the horizontal span (8.83 pixels). The vertical span is 6.80 pixels. Local foil wrinkles, adhesive contact or reflectivity could change light collection across that travel. The images supply no response map or percent sensitivity, and the four pairs cannot identify the foil as the principal source of scatter. A separate proton-imaging study illustrates how spatial response can be measured by comparison to a reference field. Its correction factors do not transfer to this target. [Archived centroids](../20261014/crosscal_fits_PolyAlDosCal_000006.csv) · [Clark et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/mp.17247)

The operator swept gate delay and duration to full-signal plateaus and set both comfortably inside the limits. That is direct evidence that the gate contained the optical pulse. The 100 µs camera gate gives no measurement of electron pulse duration for computing instantaneous dose rate. [Gate-containment record](README.md#gate-containment--operator-confirmation-2026-11-16)

### Where the 3.19% software default comes from

The application stores **3.19%** from a provisional **3.0% film scale** combined with **1.09%** from treating the two four-reading averages as independent. It can include that value in displayed Gy uncertainties. The optical fit precision, the uncertainty of the calibration factor and the accuracy transferred from the film answer different questions. A published total containing film-reading fluctuations cannot be assigned in full as a common scale and then combined with a statistical term that includes film readings. There is a risk of overlap and a missing covariance, even though slide 3 itself avoids summing those rows. A defensible replacement requires the local film curve and reference-dose budget to identify common components, and paired data for the between-shot component. There is no justified replacement total from these four measurements alone. [Calibration default and propagation](../../../dose_cam/beam_fit.py) · [JCGM §5.2](https://www.iso.org/sites/JCGM/GUM/JCGM100/C045315e-html/C045315e_FILES/MAIN_C045315e/05_e.html)

## What published EBT-XD accuracy means near 40 Gy

Ashland gives an optimal range of 0.4–40 Gy and a dynamic range extending to 60 Gy. The upper reading of 40.94 Gy lies near the upper end of the optimal range. The accuracy there depends on the local calibration range, slope, readout channel and scanner procedure. AAPM TG-235 calls for a protocol-specific uncertainty evaluation. [Manufacturer protocol](https://www.ashland.com/file_source/Ashland/Documents/PHA21-011_Gafchromic%20EBT-XD%20Protocol.pdf) · [AAPM TG-235 §6.H](https://doi.org/10.1002/mp.14497)

| Study | What was measured | Relevance to the four doses |
|---|---|---|
| León-Marroquín et al., 2019, Table II | Matched 6 MeV electron calibration and readout gave 2.3% combined reading and fitting uncertainty for red over 5–40 Gy and 2.2% for green over 8–50 Gy. Readout was at 48 h. | Covers 40 Gy directly. These figures describe the components evaluated in that protocol. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1002/mp.13708) |
| Raj et al., 2024, Table 1 | 1.52% combined standard uncertainty for their red/green protocol over 0.5–30 Gy. Approx. 3% at coverage factor 2. Films were centered on the scanner. | Establishes achievable performance through 30 Gy. Quoting their 3% expanded value as one standard deviation would double the stated standard uncertainty. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1002/pro6.1236) |
| Jung et al., 2025, Tables 1, 3 and 4 | 3.5% combined standard uncertainty at about 22 Gy including 2% delivery stability. Combining their other terms gives 2.83%. At the highest electron dose they reported film 49.6 Gy versus alanine 48.1 Gy, a +3.3% difference over 49 pulses. | The budget includes reading and scanner terms. The high-dose check used about 1 Gy per pulse. Neither figure specifies the shared scale uncertainty of the Duke film. [Primary tables](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12464357/) |
| Whitmore et al., 2024, error analysis | Approx. 5% in their focused-electron experiment. Differences between red and green channels averaged 4%, calibration modalities differed by up to 3.5%, and two definitions of peak differed by 0.6% on average. | A useful example with calibration through 40 Gy and an explicit comparison of peak estimators. Their quoted 5% has no stated coverage factor. [Primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11096185/) |

These benchmarks make **“about 3%, protocol dependent”** reasonable as context. Applying them as independent uncertainties on each of the four film points, or substituting them for the local shared film scale, would misstate what was measured.

## Does EBT-XD overrespond to one intense electron pulse?

The studies below were selected for film formulation, dose per pulse, independent dose or charge reference, and relevance to HIGS. Total dose and dose per pulse are reported separately. The search covered positive and null studies, facility reports, primary papers cited by recent studies, and adjacent evidence on other film formulations or particles. This is a targeted evidence review. It offers no estimate of unpublished null results or publication bias.

| Primary evidence | Protocol, replication and result | Weight for this measurement |
|---|---|---|
| Sherman et al., HIGS, 2026 | 11 alanine pellets, each given one pulse near 30 Gy in a 35 MeV electron beam at about 30 MGy/s. EBT-XD read at 24 h. Alanine EPR calibrated independently at conventional dose rates from 5 to 40 Gy. Average film–alanine agreement within about 2.5%. | Closest direct check in facility, pulse structure and dose. Preliminary conference abstract with no paired results or interval on the mean difference. The reported 1.8% interpellet variation refers to the calibration. It gives no bound at 40 Gy. [P51, p. 95](https://eprbiodose2026.org/doc/EPRBioDose2026_Abstract_Book_Final_Website_Version.pdf#page=95) |
| Del Sarto et al., 2025 | 9 MeV, roughly 40 Gy total, 0.7–19.3 Gy/pulse, five films per setting. Maximum inferred dose overresponse 7% ± 3.3% relative to flashDiamond in their high instantaneous-rate comparison. | The numerical ratio of effect to quoted uncertainty is about 2.1. The selected extreme, combined experimental uncertainties and reference response preclude reading that as a formal two-sided significance test. The high point was below 40 Gy/pulse. [Primary methods and discussion](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphy.2025.1474416) |
| Cayley et al., 2025 | 100 MeV, up to 23 Gy in a 200 ns train. Film response rose about 20% above a charge-based linear extrapolation. Matched total charge split across 1, 2 or 20 pulses supplied a second comparison, repeated three times with one-SEM bars. | Independent experimental support for a real effect under some conditions. Charge is a proxy for absorbed dose. Charge stability, uneven film baseline and the low-charge normalization limit a transferable correction. No formal interval for the quoted maximum. [Primary paper, Figs. 4–5](https://doi.org/10.3390/app15148101) |
| Schott et al., 2026 | At roughly 13 Gy total, three stacks per condition received 1.06, 2.19 or 3.27 Gy/pulse. No discernible trend with pulse dose. EBT-XD differed from flashDiamond by about 4.84% on average in the comparison above 1.5 Gy total. | Direct counterevidence in a lower-dose-per-pulse regime. The comparison gives no equivalence limit at 40 Gy/pulse. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejmp.2026.105723) |
| Szpala et al., 2021 | EBT-XD followed the expected geometrical dose falloff up to their reported 20,000 Gy/s. At the high-rate end the comparator was an effective inverse-square model normalized to film at long distance. OSL measurements extended to about 280 Gy/s. | Counterevidence at high rates. Its reference and changing irradiation geometry give less leverage on a percent-level absolute bias than a simultaneous, independently calibrated dosimeter. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1002/acm2.13270) |
| Rieker et al., 2025 | EBT-XD and alanine agreed within about 5% at 10–20 Gy under conventional irradiation. UHDR single trains were compared to passive dosimeters including RPL glass, with substantial experimental scatter. | The clearest alanine agreement in this study concerns conventional dose rate. The UHDR result cannot tightly bound an effect in our pulse. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphy.2025.1597079) |

**High statistics and reporting bias.** Horst et al. irradiated about 600 EBT3 samples across four campaigns and found substantial overresponse at high pulse doses, including roughly 15% at 18.5 Gy/pulse and 25% at 32.5 Gy/pulse under particular conditions. Stacked samples share irradiations, all EBT3 came from one lot, and their other tested film was OC-1. This strengthens the physical case for some radiochromic films; it cannot quantify EBT-XD response. Villoing et al. also observed an EBT-XD response change in proton beams. Neither result supplies a correction for these electrons. A pooled mean across different references, pulse structures, film models and selected positive endpoints would obscure these distinctions. I found no high-statistics, independently referenced EBT-XD study at a closely matched single electron pulse of 40 Gy that settles the size or sign here. [Horst et al.](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6560/ae47b6) · [Villoing et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/mp.15526)

**A second HIGS comparison explains why the choice of reference matters.** In a 2026 Duke master's thesis, Seth Wilcox compared BeO optically stimulated luminescence dosimeters (OSLDs) with EBT-XD for single HIGS pulses and reported a roughly 25% discrepancy. The clinical control also showed a substantial OSLD dose discrepancy at conventional rate, persisting after recalibration. Wilcox discusses OSLD calibration and energy response as contributors. The HIGS discrepancy cannot be assigned to the film. This was a separate detector experiment within the same research program as the alanine poster. [Wilcox thesis, printed pp. 12–18, especially Figs. 6–10](https://dukespace.lib.duke.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/4021ee3d-391b-423a-909d-2d8dc72910f4/content)

## Other slide checks

- **Primary Cherenkov cone.** At 30 MeV and refractive index 1.585 the half-angle is about 50.88° and the PC–air critical angle is about 39.12°. With a planar parallel specular back face the smallest incidence angle is the cone angle minus the tilt relative to the electron track. The first rays can escape at a tilt just above **11.76°**. At exactly 12° a narrow azimuthal segment can already escape at that index. Measured dispersion of bulk PC gives boundaries of about 13.3° at 436 nm, 11.8° at 588 nm and 10.9° at 804 nm. The reported few-degree tilt is comfortably within total internal reflection for ideal primary rays at those wavelengths. “The first escape is near 12°” is precise. A bulk measurement gives no light-fraction estimate for a rough taped target. [PDG Cherenkov relation](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2024/reviews/rpp2024-rev-particle-detectors-accel.pdf) · [Kasarova et al., Table 1](https://bcc.bas.bg/BCC_Volumes/Volume_47_Special_B_2015/BCC-47-SI-B-8.pdf)
- **Polycarbonate rate response.** Nakamura et al. established emission in undoped polycarbonate, including a fluorescence maximum near 350 nm and an electron-excited light-yield measurement. They did not vary FLASH pulse dose. Giguère et al. observed the onset of nonlinear response at about 45 Gy/pulse on average for the commercial plastic scintillators they studied at 200 MeV. Their materials and pulse structure differ from the thin framing-grade PC here. The available evidence leaves the rate dependence of this sheet unquantified. A fixed response at the calibration dose would be absorbed in DN/Gy. [Undoped PC experiment](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jhps/49/2/49_98/_pdf) · [VHEE scintillator study](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3Aa30ce378-0c0f-41f5-8d0e-2fca0398fe46)
- **CoreXY torque.** The 2.43 kg mass ledger and 70/30 split of the shifted subgroup reproduce the stated support reactions. An off-center mass creates a moment on the guides. How much extra shaft torque follows depends on the belt routing and guide friction. For ideal CoreXY with pure vertical external force, virtual work and the conventional motor-coordinate transform give equal drive-force magnitudes. The slide’s 0.22/0.16 N·m are conditional sizing estimates assuming each motor carries its side’s reaction. The 0.45 N·m nameplate is a holding rating. Usable running torque at the actual 12 V and selected current remains unverified. [Project mass and torque calculation](README.md#corexy-torque-estimate) · [CoreXY motor-coordinate transform](https://www.klipper3d.org/Kinematics.html#corexy-robots)
- **Alignment and planned automation.** The two DoseCam examples demonstrate feature visibility. Position-calibration accuracy remains unmeasured. Slide 7 identifies the controls illustration and distinguishes programmed pulse counts from future measured-dose feedback. Those descriptions are consistent with the archived images and interface.
